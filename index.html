The philosophy
Ch. 6

Why loads go down

A temporary configuration is exposed for months, not 50 years. ASCE 37 sizes the construction wind so it has the same chance of being exceeded during the construction period as the permanent design wind has over a 50-year life. The reduced loads only hold if the site is managed: people kept out when loads exceed the design, and snow cleared promptly.

Risk Category II

All environmental loads during construction use RC II, whatever the finished structure is, unless the AHJ says otherwise.

raise it next to a hospital or other essential facility

No 16 psf floor

The ASCE 7-10 minimum wind pressure does not have to be applied.

commentary: consider it for hazardous ops anyway

No phase-splitting

You can't cut one continuous operation into short pieces to earn a lower factor, or extend an interim condition past its original period.

Pick a construction period

The construction period runs from first erection to structural completion of each independent structural system, cladding included. The factor multiplies the ASCE 7-10 basic wind speed. Everything else in the ASCE 7-10 procedure (exposure, Kz, Kzt, Kd, G, Cf) stays the same.

speed −25% → load −44%. say this out loud in the interview

For periods under six weeks, a factor below 0.75 is allowed if a statistical analysis of local wind data for that season supports it. Separately, Sec. 6.2.1.2 covers continuously monitored work periods such as crane lifts with a stop-work wind speed. Read that text directly before the interview.

When does 115 mph apply?

On the Gulf Coast and Eastern Seaboard, where the ASCE 7-10 mapped speed exceeds 115 mph, you may substitute 115 mph (roughly the inland RC II speed). The rules depend on whether your construction window touches July 1 – October 31. Set up a job below and watch the window sweep the calendar.

Snow during construction
pf

Yes, snow counts

Snow is one of the environmental loads ASCE 37 covers. It is reduced, not dropped. Use ASCE 7-10 Chapter 7 with a reduced ground snow load, and keep checking drift, unbalanced, sliding and rain-on-snow wherever the temporary geometry creates them (forms beside a parapet, a deck step at a construction joint).

Is = 1.0 because Sec. 6.1 sets Risk Category II. The 0.8 factor on pg is how practitioners apply ASCE 37-14 for construction periods of five years or less. Combine snow with construction loads per ASCE 37 Chapter 2. Sec. 2.3.1 gives no ASD combination that pairs construction snow with other loads, so some engineers fall back on ASCE 7 combinations with construction live load treated as L.
