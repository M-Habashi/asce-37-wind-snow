Falsework and formwork fail during the pour: the load is at its maximum, the concrete is still liquid, and the system is at its least stiff. Know the design loads cold, know the load path from soffit to soil, and know the few equations that set the pressure.

Interview angle

Name the variables: rate of placement (the big one), temperature (cold concrete sets slowly, so pressure rises), unit weight, chemistry and retarders, slump and SCC, vibration depth, and element type. Control the rate in the field: write the maximum pour rate on the drawings and monitor it. A hot-weather retarder can turn a 1,100 psf design into liquid head.

Caltrans specifics worth quoting

Beam deflection from the full concrete weight (treated as one pour, falsework self-weight excluded) is limited to L/240 (48-2.02B(3); FW Manual §4-2.01). Earthquake isn't considered for falsework (§3-1). Heavy-duty shoring gets a wind shape factor of 2.2 on height-zone pressures (§3-3.03A). Wind generally governs above ~30–40 ft; below that, the 2% load governs (§3-3.01). Timber uses CD = 1.25 (§5-2.03). The AASHTO guide adds +30% impact for placement or lifting, and posts next to traffic need a 2,000 lb base / 1,000 lb top connection.

    OSHA 1926.703 in one breath

    Formwork drawings, including shoring and reshoring layouts, must be at the jobsite. Inspect shores before and during placement. Don't remove forms or shores until the concrete can carry its load, per tests or specified criteria. Reshore when slabs carry construction loads before reaching design strength.
