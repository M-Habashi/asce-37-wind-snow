A temporary bridge is still a bridge. Owners usually make you design detours and haul bridges to AASHTO LRFD, sometimes with a reduced vehicle. Work trestles add crane tracks, stream flow and scour. Erecting the permanent girders brings the hardest stability cases on the job.

Construction · Art. 3.4.2.1

Unless the owner says otherwise: the load factor on construction loads and their dynamic effects is at least 1.5 in Strength I. When you investigate Strength I, III and V during construction, DC and DW factors can't be less than 1.25 for maximized force effects. Strength IV uses the normal table values. Construction deflections are checked at Service I, with construction dead load treated as permanent load and construction transient loads as live load.

Remember

η = ηDηRηI (ductility, redundancy, importance). A two-girder temporary span or a single pile bent is non-redundant, so say so. Use the max/min factor that produces the worse effect: 0.90DC when dead load helps, as in uplift at bearings or overturning of a bent.

HL-93 · Art. 3.6.1
truck / tandem + lane

HL-93 by hand

Design truck

8 + 32 + 32 kips. Axles 14 ft apart, with the rear spacing variable from 14 to 30 ft. Use 14 ft for max moment on simple spans.

Design tandem

2 × 25 kips at 4 ft. It governs short spans, roughly under 40 ft for moment.

Lane load

0.64 klf over a 10 ft width. No impact on the lane load.

IM & m

IM = 33% (15% fatigue, 75% deck joints). Multiple presence m = 1.20 / 1.00 / 0.85 / 0.65 for 1 / 2 / 3 / >3 lanes.

Work trestle · crawler crane on a pile bent

A crane walks the trestle

Kiewit builds many river and marine bridges from work trestles: driven pipe or H-pile bents, cap beams, stringers, and timber or steel mats. The crane is the design vehicle. Its tracks put a moving, swinging trapezoid of pressure onto the stringers. The bents collect it, and the cap splits it between piles by lever arm.

What else they'll ask

Add the deck and mat dead load, the crane's travel and swing dynamics (company criteria; AASHTO's IM is for trucks, not crawlers), lateral loads (stream flow, wind on crane and load, braking), and bent bracing. Size the piles for axial capacity (wave equation, PDA/CAPWAP, set criteria), lateral resistance (p-y, scour depth!) and buckling of the free length in water. The crane stays where the stringers were designed: mark the track lines on the deck.

Don't forget

Scour lengthens the free length and removes lateral support exactly when stream force peaks. Also consider vessel and barge impact (Art. 3.14, and fendering for work barges), ice (Art. 3.9), and wave loads in marine work. Seasonal flood windows are often the real schedule constraint.

Girder erection stability · FHWA NHI-15-044
system buckling

Two girders aren't twice as stable

Cross-frames make a girder pair act as a system. Its global lateral-torsional buckling depends on the girder spacing, not on the cross-frame spacing. Yura's system buckling moment for a twin-girder unit is Mgs = Cb(π²sE/L²)√(IyIx), with I for one girder, compared against the total moment on both. Narrow, long, unbraced pairs are the danger. Adding more cross-frames doesn't fix it; a lateral (plan) bracing system, more girders, or an intermediate support does.

Fit-up

Cross-frame detailing method for curved or skewed girders: no-load fit, steel-dead-load fit, or total-dead-load fit. It decides whether the frames go in easily and whether the girders end up plumb. It's a favorite erection-engineering question.

Holding crane / towers

The first girder line often needs a holding crane, a falsework tower at the splice, or tie-backs until the second girder and cross-frames are in. Watch uplift at bearings on curved or skewed spans.

Deck pour

Overhang brackets put torsion into the exterior girders. Check temporary bracing, the pour sequence (positive-moment regions first), and the screed-machine load at the edge (75 plf plus machine wheel loads).
