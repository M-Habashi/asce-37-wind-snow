A temporary bridge is still a bridge. Detours and haul bridges are usually designed to AASHTO LRFD with HL-93 or an owner vehicle. Work trestles add crane tracks, stream flow and scour. Erecting the permanent girders creates some of the hardest stability cases on the job.

Construction · Art. 3.4.2.1

Unless the owner says otherwise: construction loads and their dynamic effects take \(\gamma\ge1.5\) in Strength I. When checking Strength I, III and V during construction, \(\gamma_{DC},\gamma_{DW}\ge1.25\) for maximized force effects. Strength IV uses the table values. Construction deflections use Service I (Art. 3.4.2.2), with construction dead load as permanent load and construction transient loads as live load.

Remember

A two-girder temporary span or a single-pile bent is non-redundant (ηR > 1.0), so say so. Use the min factor (0.90DC) when dead load helps: bearing uplift, overturning of a bent, sliding.

Don't forget

Scour lengthens the free length and removes lateral support exactly when stream force peaks. Also consider vessel and barge impact (Art. 3.14), ice (Art. 3.9) and waves in marine work. Use the construction-period design flood agreed with the owner.

Erection stability flow

Fit-up

Cross-frame detailing method for curved or skewed girders: no-load, steel-dead-load or total-dead-load fit. It sets fit-up forces during erection and how plumb the webs end up.

Holding crane / towers

The first girder line often needs a holding crane, a falsework tower at the splice, or tie-backs until the second girder and cross-frames are in. Watch uplift at bearings on curved or skewed spans.

Deck pour

Overhang brackets torque the exterior girders. Check temporary bracing, the pour sequence (positive-moment regions first), and the screed load at the edge (75 plf + machine wheel loads).
