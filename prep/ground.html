Temporary works engineers own the hole as well as the steel. That means shoring for excavations, the surcharge a crane puts behind that shoring, cofferdams that hold back a river, and the pressure a mat puts on a soft layer six feet down. OSHA 1926 Subpart P
protective systems

The trench rules

top width — ft

5 ft

A protective system is required at 5 ft or deeper, unless it's entirely in stable rock. Shallower trenches need one too if a competent person sees signs of cave-in.

20 ft

Deeper than 20 ft, sloping and shoring must be designed by a registered PE.

4 ft · 25 ft

Trenches 4 ft or deeper need a ladder or ramp within 25 ft of lateral travel.

2 ft

Keep spoil and equipment at least 2 ft back from the edge. A crane farther back is still a surcharge.

Earth pressure + surcharge · Rankine · Boussinesq
crane behind shoring

A crane next to the shoring

Active Rankine pressure plus a uniform surcharge, plus a point load such as an outrigger pad, using the modified Boussinesq solution for a rigid wall (NAVFAC / Caltrans T&S). Slide the outrigger toward the wall and watch the pressure bulb climb the shoring.

Pa —Pq —Poutrigger — k/ft

Point load on an unyielding wall, directly in line with the load: m = x/H, n = z/H. If m ≤ 0.4, σh = (Q/H²)·0.28n²/(0.16 + n²)³. If m > 0.4, σh = (Q/H²)·1.77m²n²/(m² + n²)³. Away from that plane it falls off as cos²(1.1θ). The result is per foot of wall at the peak; spreading it along the wall is a judgment call, so state what you assumed.

Remember

Ka = tan²(45° − φ/2), K0 = 1 − sin φ (at-rest, for stiff braced systems next to footings), Kp = tan²(45° + φ/2), reduced by a factor of safety for embedment. Add water pressure below the water table, using buoyant γ' for the soil plus full hydrostatic. Caltrans T&S requires a minimum lateral construction surcharge of 72 psf over the top 10 ft of any shoring, about 2 ft of soil at Ka = 0.33. It's a lateral pressure, not a vertical q. Use your owner's value.

Braced excavations · Terzaghi–Peck apparent pressure
strut loads

Apparent pressure & struts

Interview angle

Apparent pressure diagrams are envelopes back-calculated from measured strut loads, not real pressure distributions. Use them for struts and wales, not for the embedment of the wall. In soft clay, also check basal heave (FS = Nccu/(γH + q)) and the stiffness and sequence of the system. Settlement behind the wall can matter more than strength.

Cofferdams · tremie seal · piping
buoyancy

Keep the river out

A sheet-pile cofferdam with a tremie seal is a bathtub you pump dry. Once it's dewatered, the full outside head pushes up on the bottom of the seal. The seal's own weight, plus any bond to the foundation piles, has to hold it down.

Also check

Seal flexure between piles (unreinforced concrete, low allowable tension). Wale and strut rings against hydrostatic + soil. Piping or boiling at the toe: critical gradient ic = γ'/γw ≈ 1.0, so aim for FS 1.5–2 with longer sheets or relief wells. The dewatering sequence: brace each level before pumping down past it. Plan to flood the cofferdam when the river overtops it.

Bearing capacity in one line

qult = cNcsc + qNq + ½γBNγsγ. Use FS ≈ 2–3 for temporary pads, or the geotech's allowable. For saturated clay under fast crane loading, the undrained case governs: qult ≈ 5.14cu (strip) to 6.2cu (square). If you can't get a geotech value, a plate load test or proof roll gives you something real. Don't forget voids, utilities and recent backfill.

Crane on a barge · stability
GM · list

The suspended load lives at the boom tip

For marine work the barge is the foundation. Its stability is GM = KB + BM − KG, with BM = Iwp/∇. The suspended load acts at the point of suspension (the boom tip), not where it hangs. That raises KG. Swinging the load out lists the barge: tan θ ≈ w·y/(Δ·GM).
