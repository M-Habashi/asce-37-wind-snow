Sling geometry · CG · ASME B30.9
T = W / (n sin θ)

The load hangs CG-under-hook

Two slings meet at one hook. Slings, load and hook form a rigid triangle hanging from a pin, so the load rotates until its CG sits directly under the hook. If the CG isn't centered between the picks, the load tilts, and the sling closer to the CG goes steeper and carries more. Move the CG, then lengthen one sling to level it.

"Each leg carries W/2" is only true with a centered CG and equal slings. The vertical share follows the lever rule (VA = W·b/(a+b)), and then you divide by sin θ for that leg. For 4-leg bridles, assume only 2 legs carry the load unless you equalize them (spreader, equalizer plate, chain-fall). A rigid load on four slings is statically indeterminate.

Code map

ASME B30.20 is the safety standard: marking, inspection, and a proof load test of 125% of rated load before first use. ASME BTH-1 is the design standard: Design Category A (Nd = 2.00) or B (Nd = 3.00), and Service Class 0–4 by load cycles (Class 0 is up to 20,000 cycles, which covers most project-specific devices). The ASD-style checks look like AISC's but divide by Nd. The spreader check above approximates the allowable load as FcrAg/Nd, the AISC column curve divided by the BTH-1 design factor. BTH-1 has its own compression provisions, so look them up.

Stability trap

With converging slings to one hook, the load is stable as long as its CG is below the hook. With vertical slings under a spreader, the system is a parallelogram mechanism. The CG then has to sit below the pick points, or a tall load can roll over in the rigging.

Lifting lug / padeye · BTH-1 §3-3.3
pin-connected plate

Design a padeye

The most likely whiteboard question. BTH-1's pin-connected plate checks: tension through the hole (with the pin-to-hole size effect Cr and effective width), double-plane shear out past the hole, and bearing. Then the welds and the base metal it's welded to.

—
Details they'll probe

Keep the hole close to the pin size: a big Dh/Dp lowers Cr and concentrates bearing. Fill the shackle jaw with cheek plates or spacers so the shackle can't slide or bend its pin. Align the lug with the sling (no out-of-plane load), or design for a side-load allowance per your company standard. Check the base metal of the girder or flange you weld to, and look for lamellar tearing on thick plates. Have an NDE plan for the welds.

Crane capacity · OSHA 1926 Subpart CC
% of chart

Percent of chart

The chart capacity is for the exact configuration: boom length, counterweight, tracks extended, over-rear or 360°, and level, firm ground. Everything below the boom tip counts against it: hook block, rigging, spreader, jib if erected. Radius is measured from the center of rotation to the hook. A boom deflects outward under load, so the loaded radius grows.

Illustrative chart

The capacity curve here is a made-up 250-ton crawler for practice. Always read the manufacturer's chart for the actual configuration, and confirm the chart's own deductions and notes.

Critical lift

Typical company triggers: above ~75% of chart (some use 85%), tandem picks, personnel, lifts over people, energized lines or live process, and cranes on barges or structures. The exact thresholds vary by company.

Tandem

Each crane sees its share, which changes as the load tilts or a crane booms. Many programs cap each crane at ~75–80% of chart and require an engineered plan.

Personnel

OSHA 1926.1431: the platform plus load can't exceed 50% of rated capacity. Proof test and trial lift first.

Wind

Use the manufacturer's in-service wind limit. Reduce it for loads with a large sail area such as panels and forms. ASCE 37's monitored-period provisions tie into the stop-work speed.

Ground bearing · crawler tracks · mats
qmax vs qallow

What the ground sees

Treat the crawler as a rigid body on two tracks. The resultant (crane + counterweight + load) lands at an eccentricity set by the boom's direction. Along each track the pressure is a trapezoid while e ≤ L/6 and becomes a triangle beyond that. Between the tracks, the load splits by lever arm. Over the corner is usually worst.

Mats only help as far as they're stiff enough to spread the load. Check timber or steel mat bending and shear (NDS: CD, wet service), then the soil. That means a 2:1 spread down to any weak layer and bearing capacity with a factor of safety, or a plate load test. Ask about utilities, voids, backfilled trenches and excavations nearby: a track next to a trench is a lateral surcharge on its shoring (ground page).

Picking girders & precast · PCI
0.207 L

Where to put the picks

For a prismatic member picked at two points with vertical slings, the moment over the picks is negative (the cantilevers) and the moment at midspan is positive. Slide the picks in until the two are equal: at a = 0.207L each is 0.0214wL², about 1/6 of the 0.125wL² you'd get picking at the ends.

Precast (PCI)

For lateral stability while hanging, PCI's recommended practice targets a factor of safety of about 1.0 against cracking and 1.5 against failure (rollover during hauling). Lifting inserts are designed with a factor of safety (commonly 4) on the manufacturer's capacity. Add form-suction and impact multipliers when stripping.

Steel girders

A hanging girder can buckle laterally between its lifting clamps. Use spreaders or two cranes for long, slender pieces. UT Austin's "UT Lift" approach and FHWA NHI-15-044 cover it.

Inclined slings

Inclined slings without a spreader put axial compression into the member, P = T cos θ. That's harmless for a stocky piece and a buckling problem for a slender truss chord or a long precast pile.
