Sling geometry · CG · ASME B30.9
\(T=V/\sin\theta\)

The load hangs CG-under-hook

$$V_A=W\frac{b}{a+b},\qquad V_B=W\frac{a}{a+b},\qquad T_i=\frac{V_i}{\sin\theta_i}\qquad\text{symmetric: } T=\frac{W}{n\sin\theta}$$statics · ASME B30.9: horizontal sling angles below 30° only as recommended by the sling manufacturer or a qualified person

Two slings meeting at one hook form a rigid triangle hanging from a pin. The load rotates until its CG sits directly under the hook. If the CG isn't centered between the picks, the load tilts, and the leg nearer the CG goes steeper and carries more.

Stability trap

With converging slings to one hook, the load is stable while its CG is below the hook. With vertical slings under a spreader, the system is a parallelogram mechanism, so the CG has to sit below the pick points or a tall load can roll over in the rigging.

Padeye procedure

Details they'll probe

Keep Dh close to Dp: the clearance lowers Cr and concentrates bearing. Fill the shackle jaw with cheek plates or spacers. Align the lug with the sling, or design for a side load per your company standard. Check the base metal (flange local bending; lamellar tearing on thick plate). Picking girders & precast · PCI

Where to put the picks

$$M^-=\frac{wa^2}{2}\qquad M^+=\frac{w(L-2a)^2}{8}-\frac{wa^2}{2}\qquad M^-=M^+\ \Rightarrow\ a=\frac{L}{2+2\sqrt2}=0.207L,\quad M=0.0214\,wL^2\ \ (\approx\tfrac16\ \text{of}\ \tfrac{wL^2}{8})$$statics, vertical slings · add the axial compression \(P=T\cos\theta\) for inclined slings
a/L = —\(M^+\) — wL²\(M^-\) — wL²

Precast (PCI)

PCI's recommended practice for lateral stability while hanging targets FS ≈ 1.0 against cracking and 1.5 against failure (rollover when hauling). Lifting inserts commonly get FS 4 on the manufacturer's ultimate. Add form-suction and impact multipliers when stripping.

Steel girders

A hanging girder can buckle laterally between its lifting clamps. Use spreaders or two cranes for long, slender pieces. See UT Austin "UT Lift" and FHWA NHI-15-044 Ch. 5–6.

Inclined slings

Without a spreader, inclined slings push axial compression \(P=T\cos\theta\) into the member. That's harmless for a stocky piece and a buckling problem for a slender truss chord or a long pile.
